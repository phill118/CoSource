# Canonical evidence ledger

The application kernel owns one memory-only product evidence ledger keyed exclusively by canonical provider-scoped product identity. Discovery workspaces contain temporary candidates; accepted search responses, including agent searches and overflow, do not mutate retained evidence. Human inspection, comparison selection, or plan addition can retain a candidate. Refresh is an explicit kernel operation and cannot change goal commitment, comparison selection, plan lines, plan revision, proposals, or approval.

Each accepted observation records an application-generated identity, monotonic per-subject sequence, provider origin, strongest provenance category, and `observedAt`. This is when CoSource accepted the response, not a provider publication time. The current observation projects `retainedProducts`; no service owns another retained copy. Up to five superseded observations are retained newest-first, with deterministic oldest-first eviction. At most 50 product subjects may be retained. The canonical retain operation returns an explicit capacity outcome: a new subject is rejected before inspection, provider access, or refresh lifecycle creation, without evicting accepted evidence. Already-retained subjects remain inspectable and refreshable at capacity, and their sequence and bounded history continue normally. The ledger resets with the browser session.

Freshness is `current`, `stale`, `unknown`, or `not_constrained`. It is evaluated against the active Resource Requirement using the injected clock. Exact maximum-age boundaries are current; later instants are stale. `requiredAsOf` requires an observation at or after that instant. Missing, invalid, or future times are unknown. CoSource Purchasing currently projects no freshness constraint, so it reports `not_constrained`, not a guarantee. Uncommitted draft edits do not affect this policy.

Supersession is not conflict. A newer same-subject observation replaces the usable projection while bounded history remains auditable. Price or availability changes over time, merchant price differences, variant differences, and missing fields are not automatically conflicts. Product option collections describe selectable alternatives, not simultaneous product claims. A selected merchant offer's `selectedOptions` are applicable evidence for that offer; without an applicable selection, a condition requiring one variant value remains insufficient rather than conflicting. Product attributes remain product-level claims. A current conflict exists only when equally authoritative, simultaneously applicable structured claims disagree and no unique value can be established.

Decision readiness is qualitative: `blocked_by_known_failure`, `blocked_by_known_conflict`, `insufficient_evidence`, `verification_required`, or `ready_on_current_evidence`. Known mandatory failures are distinct from unresolved equal-authority evidence conflicts. Missing or stale mandatory evidence is insufficient. Inferred mandatory evidence requires verification. Preference gaps do not become mandatory failures, and no-condition candidates receive no artificial confidence. Comparison and plan evaluation reuse these meanings.

Agents may read bounded summaries but cannot retain evidence, assert verification, dismiss conflict, override freshness, approve decisions, or purchase. Shopify Global Catalog remains the only live provider. Persistence, monitoring, autonomous verification, and execution remain future work.

Evidence contracts and the product ledger live under `src/evidence`, upstream of evaluation, comparison, and planning. The kernel stores per-product refresh lifecycle and a monotonic request authority. Concurrent refreshes are last-request-wins: late success or failure from a superseded request cannot mutate evidence or replace the current lifecycle state. React only renders this canonical state.
