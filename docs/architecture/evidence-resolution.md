# Canonical evidence resolution

Evidence resolution is a derived, provider-neutral projection over CoSource's existing authorities. The retained evidence ledger continues to own accepted product observations, stable observation identity and sequence, supersession, acceptance time, and bounded history. Product evaluation owns field-level resolution and conflicts; the resource requirement owns freshness policy; canonical costing owns cost uncertainty; supplier intelligence owns quotation, reliability, and risk evidence; comparison and the single plan evaluator own their respective readiness conclusions. The application kernel composes those results. React and WebMCP do not create a second truth store.

An evidence issue has a deterministic identity derived from workspace, requirement revision, exact subject, field, and closed issue kind. Kinds are `missing`, `stale`, `conflicting`, `verification_required`, `unresolved_cost`, and `unavailable`. Importance is mandatory, preference, or informational. Mandatory issues sort before preferences and informational warnings; preference issues never become blockers. Resolution routes are closed and advertise only implemented review, refresh, human-verification, supplier-review, goal, comparison, or plan destinations.

Issues are recomputed and are not persisted as mutable truth. Candidate issue identity includes the accepted observation ID, sequence, and timestamp, so a semantically identical later observation creates a new current issue. Human verification is persisted separately, bounded to 100 records, and bound to the exact workspace, requirement revision, canonical issue snapshot, subject, and field. A confirmation or rejection also carries an application-owned immutable snapshot of the accepted observation plus the exact claim field, value, and provenance. A later observation therefore cannot inherit or appear resolved by the decision. Confirmation promotes only that claim to human-verified decision evidence. Rejection excludes only the named claim from the decision projection and recomputes from any claims that remain. The canonical issue projection reconciles the original issue against that same result: resolved history is non-blocking and exposes no stale actions. If every current claim is rejected, that resolved conflict history is accompanied by one active missing-evidence issue. A recorded fact is accepted only for a missing issue and is evaluated with the same equality, boolean, numeric, and unit semantics as provider evidence; free text does not become satisfied merely because text was entered. `requires_verification` remains unresolved. Provider observations and conflicting claims remain intact and retain their original attribution.

Verification time and optional validity are application-owned ISO instants. Future verification is inapplicable, validity endpoints are inclusive, and expired verification cannot establish resolution. A newer requirement revision makes older verification inapplicable without deleting history. Invalid and stale submissions fail before canonical state changes. Semantic duplicate submissions do not create another revision or activity.

The existing per-product refresh operation remains the only product refresh authority, including its last-request-wins token and accepted-evidence preservation on failure. The Evidence Resolution interface exposes its bounded progress and errors without provider envelopes or internal details. Merchant checkout remains the final payable-cost verification; evidence resolution adds no communication, ordering, checkout, payment, project-management, or storage-management authority.

The verification-aware product result is the single input to candidate, comparison, and purchase-plan decisions. React and WebMCP read those same results, so an accepted contradictory fact becomes a known failure rather than a false ready state. Verification never changes plan contents or revision. Issue projection also reports canonical plan-line availability, unresolved plan cost and budget evidence, supplier quotation and reliability gaps, and active or overdue supplier risks without recalculating those domains.

The version-one workspace envelope remains unchanged. Older records may omit `humanVerifications`; restoration treats that as an empty history. Each candidate-condition verification carries an application-owned semantic snapshot: condition collection and complete condition, canonical raw issue kind and ordered claim set, applicable offer, and observation binding. For the current revision, restoration requires that snapshot to equal the actual condition in its exact collection, then invokes the same provider-neutral single-condition evaluator used by live product evaluation. The resulting issue kind, ordered claims, applicable offer, selected claim, and deterministic issue ID must all agree; recalculating an ID from forged stored fields is insufficient. Valid earlier-revision history remains retained and inapplicable even after its condition is removed. When a legitimately old bound observation falls outside the contiguous five-entry evidence window, internally consistent snapshot history remains valid only if its sequence is strictly older than the window. Confirmed and rejected decisions still require a non-empty, unique, canonically ordered claim set and an exact selected claim. Recorded missing facts remain claimless, retain their required human value, and continue under their requirement-revision and validity policy. A claimless `requires_verification` marker remains historical and cannot improve readiness. A missing binding within the retained sequence range, or any future, impossible, cross-observation, non-applicable-offer, or mismatched semantic binding, is corrupt. Corrupt records enter existing recovery without rewriting stored bytes. Restoration performs no provider request.

WebMCP remains exactly 13 tools. `get_evidence_gaps` includes bounded canonical issue summaries, exact claim references, current resolution status, and resulting readiness, while no tool can confirm, reject, record, dismiss, or otherwise mutate human verification.
